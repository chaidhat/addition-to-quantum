His fundamental assumption was that particles's momentum and wavelength were inversely proportional.
$$
\lambda = \frac{h}{p}
$$
i.e.,
$$
\Leftrightarrow p=\hbar k
$$
We also experimentally found out that a momentum-p [[eigenstate|eigenstate]] has a single, [[definite]] wavelength.

Let's write the momentum [[wave-function]] $\psi_p(x)$ in polar form.
$$
\psi_p(x)=A(x)e^{i\theta (x)}
$$
Since we know that the wave number is [[definite]],
$$
\frac{d\theta}{dx}=k=\text{const}
$$
we [[integration|integrate]] that to get
$$
\theta(x) = kx + \theta_0
$$
Because definite momentum forces the [[magnitude]] of the wave to be constant i.e.,
$$
|\psi_p(x)|^2 = A(x)^2 = \text{const}
$$
hence
$$
A(x) = A_0 = \text{const}
$$
hence
$$
\psi_p(x) = A_0\,e^{i(kx+\theta_0)} = \underbrace{A_0\,e^{i\theta_0}}_{=\,C}\,e^{ikx}
$$

In position space, a momentum eigenstate must look like a plane wave
$$
\psi_p(x)=\braket{x|p}=Ce^{ipx/\hbar}
$$
